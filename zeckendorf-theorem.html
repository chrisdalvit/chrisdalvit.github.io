Formalized Zeckendorf Theorem

19. November 2024

This blog post presents my formalized proof of the Zeckendorf Theorem using the theorem prover Isabelle/HOL. First I'm going to describe the original proof and then I will discuss my formalization. The formal proof is available on the Archive of Formal Proofs and GitHub.

Proof

Informally, the Zeckendorf theorem states that every positive integer can be represented uniquely as the sum of one or more distinct Fibonacci numbers in such a way that the sum does not include any two consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Fibonacci numbers are a sequence of natural numbers defined as

F_0 = 0 \qquad F_1 = 1 F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}

for n > 1 . More precisely the theorem states, if N is any positive integer, there exist positive integers c_i \ge 2 , with c_{i+1} > c_i+1 , such that

N = \sum_{i=0}^k F_{c_i}

Let's now walk through the original proof from the Dutch mathematician Gerrit Lekkerkerker

"Voorstelling van natuurlijke getallen door een som van getallen van Fibonacci"
Gerrit C. Lekkerkerker, 1951 (Accessed 21.12.2024)
[1] . We prove the theorem by induction over N . For N=1 we choose c_0 = 2 and have

1 = \sum_{i = 0}^0 F_{c_i} = F_{c_0} = F_2 = 1

It is obvious that this representation is unique. Now we can continue by assuming that the Zeckendorf theorem holds for 1,\dots,N-1 and proving the theorem for N . We can observe that every positive natural number is either a Fibonacci number or lies between two consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Therefore, every N > 0 there exists a Fibonacci number F_i , such that

\begin{equation} F_i \le N < F_{i+1} \end{equation}

If N is not Fibonacci number, the first inequality is strict. If N is a Fibonacci number there is nothing to prove (since N can be represented as a sum with F_i as single element). Therefore, we will continue assuming N is not a Fibonacci number. By substracting F_i from equation (1) we get 0 < N - F_i < F_{i+1} - F_i . By the definition of Fibonacci numbers we can rewrite the inequality as 0 < N - F_i < F_{i-1} . From equation (1) and the fact that the Fibonacci sequence is strictly increasing we get F_{i-1} < N .

Trivially, we also have N = F_i + (N - F_i) .
