Formalized Zeckendorf Theorem

19. November 2024

This blog post presents my formalized proof of the Zeckendorf Theorem using the theorem prover Isabelle/HOL. First I'm going to describe the original proof and then I will discuss my formalization. The formal proof is available on the Archive of Formal Proofs and GitHub.

Proof

Informally, the Zeckendorf theorem states that every positive integer can be represented uniquely as the sum of one or more distinct Fibonacci numbers in such a way that the sum does not include any two consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Fibonacci numbers are a sequence of natural numbers where

F_0 = 0 \qquad F_1 = 1 \qquad F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}

for n > 1 . More precisely, if N is any positive integer, there exist positive integers c_i \ge 2 , with c_{i+1} > c_i+1 , such that

N = \sum_{i=0}^k F_{c_i}

Let's now walk through the original proof from the Dutch mathematician Gerrit Lekkerkerker. While the theorem is named by Edouard Zeckendorf who published a paper in 1972, the same result had been published 20 years earlier by Lekkerkerker
